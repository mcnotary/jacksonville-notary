Signing Requirements

Mandatory Client Prep Checklist:

  1. Valid Government Photo ID: Every signer must present an unexpired, government-issued photo identification item (Florida Driver’s License, US Passport, or military ID cards).
  2. Unsigned Documents: Do not sign or fill out execution date blanks prior to meeting. All documents must be signed directly in the physical or digital presence of the notary public.
  3. Complete Text: Documents must contain full, readable legal text. A notary public is legally prohibited from processing partial or incomplete signature pages.
  4. Witnesses Requirements: Certain deeds and wills require independent witnesses. Ensure your required witnesses are available at the site, hold valid photo IDs, and are entirely unrelated to the underlying document transaction.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an Apostille and standard notarization?
A standard notarization verifies identity for internal domestic paperwork. An Apostille is an extended, international legal certification verified by the Florida Department of State, rendering the verified document legal for international use among member nations of the Hague Convention.
Can a notary prepare my legal forms or give legal advice?
No. Florida Notaries are strictly forbidden by state statute from drafting legal text, advising on document choices, or interpreting legal rights. For document preparation guidance, please seek advice from a licensed attorney.
What are the identity requirements for standard Florida notarizations?
Signers must present a current, unexpired ID showing a distinct photograph, matching signature, and descriptive serial identifier. Expired IDs cannot be accepted under Florida compliance regulations unless backed by official corporate extensions.
How does a Remote Online Notarization (RON) appointment work?
During a RON session, signers complete secure identity verification checks online (including knowledge-based identity questions and biometric ID scans), then join an encrypted live video call to electronically sign the paperwork while the notary digital stamps the form.
